# Artbook guide

The Artbook is a reference board built into Asset Manager: an endless canvas where you collect, arrange
and study images while you work. This guide walks through a first board. Every control is listed in the
[Read Me](../README.md#the-artbook) (also under Settings > Guide) and in Settings > General > Shortcuts.

## 1. Open the Artbook

Click **View** at the top right and pick **Artbook View** (or press **Ctrl+2**). To keep your library
beside it, pick **Split View** (**Ctrl+3**). To keep the board over another app, such as Blender or
Photoshop, pick **Separate View** (**Ctrl+4**): the board gets its own window, and its **Window** button
has Always on Top, Opacity and Click-through.

Your boards are called **pages**. The menu button at the top left (**Ctrl+B**) opens the Pages sidebar,
where you make, rename, sort and delete pages, and keep them in folders.

## 2. Bring in images

Any of these work:

- Drag images from the asset grid, from Explorer, or from a web browser (search results too).
- Paste with **Ctrl+V**: a copied image, files, or an image link.
- In the asset grid, right-click > **Send to Artbook** to send the selected images to any page.
- Import a PureRef board (`.pur`) or a shared `.artbook` file with the import button at the top of the
  Pages sidebar, or by dropping the file on the board. PureRef boards keep their layout.

Images from your library stay *linked* to their files, and the page also keeps a small copy in case a file
moves. Images pasted or dropped from the web are stored in the page.

## 3. Move around

- **Zoom:** the mouse wheel, toward the cursor.
- **Pan:** middle-drag, or hold Space and drag.
- **Frame:** **F** fits the selection (or everything) on screen. Double-click an image to zoom to it.

Big boards stay quick. Zoomed out, each image shows a small copy, and the ones you look at get sharper as
you zoom in, all the way to the full picture.

## 4. Lay out the board

- Drag an image to move it. Drag a corner or side handle to scale it, or the round handle to rotate it
  (hold Shift to snap to 15 degrees).
- **A** arranges the selection (or everything) into tidy rows. Right-click > **Images** has Align,
  Distribute, Stack and Match Size.
- **Ctrl+G** groups the selection so it moves as one block, with a soft panel behind it. Select the group to
  pick the panel's colour or lock it in place.
- **]** and **[** bring to front and send to back. **H** and **V** flip.

Every change can be undone with **Ctrl+Z**.

## 5. Study your references

- **C** crops an image without losing any of it: you can always reset the crop.
- **G** shows the selected images in grayscale, and **Shift+G** the whole board, to judge values.
- **I** turns on the colour picker: click anything on the board to copy its colour's hex code.
- Right-click > Images > Selection has Opacity, Pixelated (sharp pixels for pixel art) and Caption.

## 6. Add your own notes and marks

- **T** adds a note where the cursor is. Type straight away; select the note for a bar with colours,
  highlights, fonts and sizes.
- **D** draws freehand strokes, and **E** switches to the eraser. Smoothing (on to start with, in the bar
  at the top) steadies lines drawn with a mouse. Press D again, or Esc, to stop.

## 7. Keep and share pages

Pages save themselves as you work. To keep them somewhere else, such as a synced OneDrive or Dropbox folder,
choose a folder in Settings > General > **Artbook**: your pages are moved there safely.

- **Ctrl+E** exports the selection, or the whole board, as a PNG.
- Right-click a page in the sidebar > **Export Page...** saves it as one `.artbook` file, images included,
  that anyone with Asset Manager can import.
- **L** lists every image on the page. If some files have moved, **Relink...** finds them in their new folder.
