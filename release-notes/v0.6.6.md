## What's new in Asset Manager 0.6.6

### Render button in the 3D viewer
**Render** (**F12**) makes a PNG of the model you're looking at, with the same angle, zoom, HDRI, exposure, colour, material and metal roughness.

- **Real light with [LuxCore](https://luxcorerender.org/).** A free path tracer: cast shadows, and light bouncing between parts of a model, which the viewer can't do.
- **Sizes and quality.** 720p, 1080p or 1440p, with **Draft** (5 s), **Good** (20 s) or **Best** (60 s) of rendering, and a **Denoise** toggle. Your choices are remembered.
- **See it as it goes.** A live preview, and **Stop** finishes early and keeps what's been rendered.
- **Downloads once.** LuxCore isn't part of the app: the first time you use it, it downloads from PyPI (157 MB, about 300 MB unpacked) and every file is checked before it's installed. **Remove LuxCore** deletes it.
- **A separate process.** A render runs apart from the app, so a graphics crash can't take your library with it.
- **The first render on a computer** prepares for its graphics card, which takes a couple of minutes, once.

On an RTX 4070, a 1080p Draft of a 235,000-triangle model took 9 seconds from click to finished picture, and a 14-million-triangle sculpt at 1440p Good took 33 seconds.

### The Viewer renderer
The **Viewer** option makes the same picture you see on screen, at any of the sizes, instantly and with no download, but without cast shadows. If LuxCore can't run on your computer, the window says why and offers this in one click.

**LuxCore is only tested on an NVIDIA card so far.** On other computers it may work, or you may need the Viewer renderer.

### What it doesn't do yet
- Textures aren't used: models render in the viewer's colour and material.
- Toon and Normals can't be rendered, as they aren't real-world materials. **Render** is greyed out for them.
- LuxCore always renders shaded, even when the viewer is showing a wireframe.
- The 3D view of an alpha has no **Render** button.

### Also changed
The 3D viewer's toolbar can now take three rows: **Render** joins **UV**, **Auto Rotate** and **Reset View** on the second, and the Roughness and Exposure sliders have a row of their own when they apply.
